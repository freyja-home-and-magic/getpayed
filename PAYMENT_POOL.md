# Payment pool

A payment pool is defined for users of the HomeVentory app ecosystem. The purpose of this pool is to incentivize the growth of both app installs, and participants in community-oriented supply of goods and services. For the reasons outlined below, the paymet pool is structured in a way that is intended to maximize fairness across members of the system while directly providing for the most legal approach to a payment pool that benefits users. 

There is a lot that goes into paying large groups of people in a fair and legal way. This is an attempt to explain what is going on in plain language.

## Motivation

HomeVentory's mission is to help our users understand and protect their homes. One of the most important aspects of being in a home as part of a community is contributing to that community. Technology has made this harder and harder as more and more things that have been traditionally handled on a local community level have been outsourced to giant corporations based outside of our communities. This is anchored by the "free" ad-supported internet. The only way to compete with free is to pay people money to use things. Thus the need for the HomeVentory payment pool.

## How it works

Purchases made from referral links, which are both made by users and contained within shared user pages, are granted a large number (a 128-bit integer in computer-speak). This number encodes, amongst other things, the pay period of the number. At the end of the pay period, currently starting at a month, these numbers are reviewed and used to pay out from the pool to users. 

There is a simple algorithm for these payments. An average based on total users is calculated, and then a cap is placed at two standard deviations from that average. Funds recouped from placing that cap are shared in a step-wise fashion with users within the rest of the distribution. 

After the pay period, users retain the numbers, but they no longer act in any fashion in an ongoing concern, monetarily or otherwise, as promisory objects for future payments from HomeVentory. HomeVentory will provide a set of APIs for the transfer and reading of possession of these numbers by 3rd parties. This opens the possibility of a secondary market for these numbers. HomeVentory will not provide any APIs for the "transfer" of these numbers to another system.

## Income, legal, and tax considerations

The goal here is to not mint millionaires, but to provide modest income as people bring cashflow back to their communities. 

The federal requirement for the reporting of taxable income from "gigs" has been raised recently from $600 to $2000. Once $2000 is reached, 1099 reporting is required on all payments. If we hit that threshold on monthly payments, we feel we'll all be quite happy with the monthly tax reporting.

These integers have an element of randomness to them, but that randomness has nothing to do with the payment pool. This is intentional as that would make the integers a form of gambling. Instead in the payment pool they act as a tally, and the secondary market is free to ascribe to them whatever value it may hold for them. In this way they act like trading cards, or a similar collectible, which derives its value from cultural activities outside of HomeVentory.

Referrals of referrals are not tracked as that would trigger multi-level marketing laws and considerations. Endeavoring to setup a MLM scheme on one's behalf is against community rules, and may result in actions up to and including exclusion from the platform. 

Because people can do whatever they want on the internet, we need to reserve the right to not pay people for whatever reason.
